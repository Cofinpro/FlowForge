## Product-Methodik: Refinement

_Ort: notebook:The Product - Business Design_

**Task: "Akzeptanzkriterien in Given-When-Then formulieren"** (QA). The Three Amigos result
(rules, edge and negative cases) goes in; testable scenarios come out.

### Procedure

1. Write the criteria in the form "Given <context>, when <some action is done>, then <a set of
   observable behaviors happens>". Given-When-Then makes it easy to tell when a story is completely
   implemented, but the When and Then blocks easily turn prescriptive; stay with goals and
   requirements [^1].
2. Write them when story and details are discussed, and again at the start of the iteration before
   programming begins; add new ones whenever they turn up later. The criteria are agreed before
   coding, because they communicate the customer's assumptions to the developers [^2][^3].
3. Give enough detail to make clear how to test that the story is done, no more [^4][^5]. Keep UI
   and technology assumptions out of the story and its tests; the UI comes up in the conversation [^6].
4. Stop when further scenarios no longer clarify the intent. Do not write a scenario per data variation:
   if all cards are processed identically, one invalid card type is enough; boundary checks such as
   February 30 belong in the developers' unit tests [^7][^8].
5. Pair a specific, repeatable action with a measurable expected outcome so the scenario can fail [^9].

**Checklist** (the notebook's synthesis of these passages): testable and objective; benefit visible to
user or purchaser, not an internal task; agreed before coding; free of UI and technology details;
action with measurable outcome; concise, no duplicates of unit tests.

### Pitfalls

- Untestable wording: "easy to use", "never wait long for any screen" (not testable, "never" and no
  defined limit) [^10].
- Vague wording: "should" (optional?) and "prominently display" mean different things to author,
  coder and tester [^11].
- Too much detail too early, UI-bound scenarios, one scenario per permutation [^4][^6][^8].
- Fake stories about team needs ("As a QA, I want database restarts automated"): hardly any acceptance
  criteria can be defined for them [^12].
