## Product-Methodik: Story formulieren

_Ort: notebook:The Product - Business Design_

**Task: "Story unter Epic in User Story Map einordnen".** Inputs: the clarified story, its epic, the existing story map. Output: the story placed under a backbone step.

### Procedure

1. **Find the backbone step (horizontal axis).** Break activities into high-level steps ordered left to right from the customer's view. The steps must not imply a technology or a solution [^1].
2. **Place the story under the step it contributes to.** Stories are grouped in the grid by the activity or workflow step they support [^2].
3. **Set the vertical position** (release/priority). Treat it as an option: check whether a partial or manually assisted first version can ship value earlier [^3].
4. **Map user activities, not system parts.** Story maps are for key user activities, not for software solutions [^4].

### Edge cases and pitfalls

- **No backbone step fits:** this usually exposes a hole in the story (a missing step). Add the step to the backbone instead of calling it scope creep [^5]. Also check that the story is a real need, not a pet feature behind a generic role [^6].
- **Story spans several steps:** it is an epic or compound story. Split it into smaller stories under their own steps [^7], each an end-to-end "slice of the cake", not a technical layer [^8].
- **Cross-cutting concerns** (capacity, performance, security) do not fit a single step. Handle them as constraint cards or tests, not as a user story under one activity [^9].
- **Do not confuse milestones with epics.** "Epic" is misused for whole topics such as "reporting"; an epic need not be delivered completely before another starts [^10].
- **Fake stories** from a system viewpoint ("As an LDAP server") are a sign of mapping by component [^11].

### Not answered by the notebook

How to pick between two equally plausible epics, and how the map itself is stored or versioned. Both stay a decision for the Product Owner.
