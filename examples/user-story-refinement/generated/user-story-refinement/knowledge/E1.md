## Product-Methodik: Definition of Ready

_Ort: notebook:The Product - Business Design_

**Checklist** (one pass/fail question each; items 1-8 are the INVEST attributes plus role and solution neutrality [^1]):

1. Concrete role or persona, not "As a user" or a system component? [^2]
2. Real user/business value (or an explicit learning goal), not a task only developers value? [^3]
3. Independent of other unfinished stories? [^1]
4. Negotiable: a placeholder for a conversation, not a fixed specification? [^4]
5. Understood well enough to estimate within the current architecture? [^5]
6. Small enough to finish and test in one sprint? [^1]
7. Objective acceptance criteria (Given-When-Then) that allow a clear pass/fail? [^6]
8. States the need without dictating screens, database design or technology? [^7]
9. Non-functional impact (performance, security, capacity) named or ruled out? The sources say user stories are poorly suited to cross-cutting concerns, so the check must ask for it explicitly [^8].
10. Measurable target or expected behaviour change stated (hypothesis, not just a requirement)? [^9]

**What to do when a criterion fails** (one remedy per criterion, notebook answer):

| Failed | Remedy | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Not independent | Combine the interdependent stories, or re-split vertically as a "slice of cake" through all layers | [^10] |
| Not negotiable | Strip rigid specification and UI assumptions; the card is a reminder to talk, not a contract | [^4] |
| Not estimatable | Time-boxed spike (learning story with an explicit goal), then the real story | [^5][^11] |
| Too large | Split the compound story into smaller stories | [^12] |
| Not testable | Replace vague wording ("easy to use") by measurable acceptance tests, or by constraint cards | [^6] |
| No concrete role | Do user role modeling; name a segment, e.g. "teacher" | [^2] |
| Solution-prescribing | Keep the "In order to…" need, drop the "I want…" solution, let the team propose options | [^7] |

**Gate outcomes** the checklist feeds: unclear criteria go back to refinement; technical risk goes to a
spike that has acceptance criteria and a time box before it starts, then back to refinement [^11].

**Pitfalls**: the check must not turn into a stage gate that demands complete specs and sign-offs
("agile-fall") [^13]; criteria are reminders for the ongoing 3-Amigos conversation, not a contract [^14].

**Not answered by the notebook**: how many criteria must pass for "ready" and any scoring or weighting
of the items; the sources give attributes and remedies, no threshold.
