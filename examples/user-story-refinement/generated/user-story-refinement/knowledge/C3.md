## Product-Methodik: Story formulieren

_Ort: notebook:The Product - Business Design_

**Task: "Bezug zu Epic, Fachkonzept & Feedback dokumentieren".** Output: the story carries its links up to the triggering feedback, the Fachkonzept role/section and the epic.

### What to record

- **Upstream origin (Pre-RS traceability):** link to what led to the requirement, e.g. the stakeholder need or business case [^1][^2]. For a ticket: the problem, the desired outcome and the customer feedback and data around the problem [^3].
- **Attributes on the story:** unique ID, name, description, stability, owner, source and author [^4]. The notebook's mapping onto the story ticket (ID, source, epic link as parent, cross-references to Fachkonzept sections, description with acceptance criteria) is its own synthesis, not a quoted template.
- **Cross-references** name the relations to other requirements [^5].
- **Hierarchy:** epic (and above it feature/theme) as the parent of the story [^6]. Linking stories to business milestones ties the work to higher-level objectives [^7].

### Keeping links maintainable

- Simplest form: a textual reference or hyperlink to the target artifact, built on unique IDs [^8].
- A matrix does not scale: a matrix of refinement relations over 2000 requirements already has 4 million cells [^9].
- Record only links that serve a defined purpose [^10].
- Do not number cards in sub-levels ("13.1, 13.2"): it adds pointless overhead [^11].

### Pitfalls

- A story describes how a system changes over time, not how it works now. After delivery, move specs and tests to a per-feature organisation and drop the stories [^12][^13]. The sources therefore stress upstream links more than permanent downstream ones; the notebook also lists post-RS links (story to code, tests) as part of full traceability.
- A story pasted into a ticket as a hand-over document from the business loses its purpose as a conversation token [^14].
