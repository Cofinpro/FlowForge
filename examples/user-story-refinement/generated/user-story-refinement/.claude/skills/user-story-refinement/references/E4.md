## Product-Methodik: Plausibilität & Freigabe

Contents: Who decides · Criteria · What must be recorded · What makes a story eligible for the Ready column · Pitfalls

**Task:** "Story priorisieren & in Ready-Spalte stellen" (PO), a human checkpoint. Claude prepares a proposed
position and its inputs; the PO decides. The gateway criteria and the WSJF summary are in
`phase-6-plausibilitaet-freigabe.md`; this file adds what the decision itself needs.

**Who decides**

- The Product Owner alone orders the backlog; one person, not a committee [^1][^2]. Where developers and the
  customer disagree on the sequence, the customer wins; developers give input, not the verdict [^3][^4].
- Even when developers want the riskiest story first, the decision stays with the customer [^5].
- What the PO needs from the team beforehand: an estimate per story ("approximately how long each story will
  take") [^3], and the developers' assumptions, alternatives and technical risk [^6].

**Criteria**

- Value and cost: the cost of a story is the developers' estimate; a story cannot be prioritised without it [^7].
- Risk and deferral impact: "the risk that the story cannot be completed as desired", "the impact the story will
  have on other stories if deferred" [^8]. Cohesiveness with other stories and desirability to broad or
  important user groups [^9].
- Time criticality: deadlines (e.g. regulatory) enter as a high Time Criticality score in the Cost of Delay
  [^10][^11].
- Method (suggest, the PO confirms): WSJF = Cost of Delay / Job Size [^12]; CoD = User Value + Business Value +
  Time Criticality + Risk Reduction + Opportunity Enablement [^13]; Job Size from the team's story points out of
  refinement [^14]. WSJF can keep scoring a changing backlog (adjust existing, score new items) [^15]. Simple
  alternative: value / cost on an exponential scale (1, 2, 4, 8, 16), "a good starting point" before other factors
  [^16]. Kano, Wiegers and dot voting: see the phase file.

**What must be recorded**

- A specific rank (first, second, third ...), not groups like "high" [^17]; each item an individual priority, plus
  rationale ("Begründung") and relations to other items (predecessor, successor, dependencies) [^18].
- The reason behind the assumptions of the rating [^19]; assumptions "explicit, visible, and recorded alongside the
  work" [^20]. Making the cost of delay explicit counters decisions by "the most senior person in the room" [^21].
- The backlog is rank-ordered and normally frozen; a new item "melts the ice", the order is rearranged, then
  frozen again [^22].

**What makes a story eligible for the Ready column**

- Ready = "selected from the backlog and ready for development"; the backlog is sorted in priority order [^23].
  Goal: at least one sprint's worth of fully groomed backlog [^24].
- Well written and understood by the team [^25]; acceptance criteria present [^26]; estimable [^27]; fits one
  iteration, otherwise split [^28][^29]; independent, no overlap in concept [^30].
- The PO prioritises, the team selects and commits; it picks from the top of the order [^31][^32]. Refinement
  (PO and team together) estimates and defines the order [^33].

**When the plausibility check found affected stories** (P4, P5)

- Impact analysis covers all affected requirements, "including the new ones", and the effort of the whole change;
  accepted changes are then prioritised and assigned to a release [^34][^35].
- Overlapping stories can be combined for planning, because estimates change with implementation order [^36]. A
  high-priority story depending on a low-priority one hampers planning [^37][^38]. A research story goes into an
  earlier iteration than the stories it unblocks [^39]. Re-estimate when new information arrives [^40]; new items
  trigger triage and re-prioritisation [^41]. Items outside the current focus are "not now", not "no" [^42].
- Inferred for this workflow: the PO sees the P4/P5 findings when positioning the story and orders it together
  with its affected and dependent stories; Claude only proposes.

**Pitfalls**

- Developers pushing infrastructural or architectural work higher than it should be [^43]; prioritising such
  tasks as stories is "pointless" [^44].
- Decision by seniority instead of an explicit economic basis [^21].
