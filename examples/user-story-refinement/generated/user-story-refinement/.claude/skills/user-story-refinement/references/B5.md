## Product-Methodik: Fachliche Klärung

_Ort: notebook:The Product - Business Design_

_Footnotes name the notebook source. The verbatim quotes and FAQ references stay with the generation output (`knowledge/`), which is not installed._

Contents: Evidence to put in front of the PO · Interpreting the evidence · Recording the decision · Pitfalls the notebook names

Evaluate the findings of the spike and/or the prototype test and decide whether they carry the story. This
is a human checkpoint: Claude assembles the evidence and proposes, the PO decides. The notebook composes
the procedure from experiment-design and discovery sources; no source describes a step by this name. The
gate criteria for "Tragen die Erkenntnisse die Story?" (users confirm problem and solution, spike shows
feasibility at acceptable cost, behaviour change plausible) are in `phase-2-fachliche-klaerung.md` (Decision
criteria); this file adds how to read the evidence and how to record the decision.

**Evidence to put in front of the PO**:

- Spike: whether the acceptance criteria defined beforehand were met, plus the spike team's recommendation
  on fit and learning curve [^1]. Spikes generate strong evidence because the code is representative of the
  bigger solution [^1]. The acceptance criteria and time box are fixed before the spike starts [^2].
- Success criteria stated up front, as a falsifiable hypothesis: "[Specific Repeatable Action] will
  [Expected Measurable Outcome]" [^3].
- Prototype test: what users did, not what they said [^4]; results tracked per participant, for example as
  share of participants who completed a task or complained about a missing feature [^5]; raw observations
  captured during the session on a testing sheet or feedback capture grid [^6].

**Interpreting the evidence**:

1. Separate strong from weak evidence: "Evidence does not speak on its own" [^7]. Weak evidence (what people
   say, feature requests, support quotes) is enough to find a direction, strong evidence is needed to
   validate it [^8][^4].
2. Look for evidence that invalidates the hypothesis before looking for what confirms it [^9]; involve
   others in the synthesis and run competing hypotheses [^10].
3. Small samples: four or five people give a good sense of major usability flaws [^11]; the result is a
   signal, not statistics [^12]; read patterns, not outliers [^13].
4. Do not let the investment decide: sunk cost makes teams "ignore the data and deploy the product as is" [^14].

**Deciding**. The notebook names persevere, pivot and kill, and "another
experiment" when the insight is unclear [^15][^16][^17]:

- Evidence supports the hypothesis: persevere, test the next critical hypothesis or the same one with a
  stronger experiment [^15][^18]. In this diagram: "Ja" to `Merge_Einordnung`.
- Evidence refutes it, or the profit or value potential is insufficient: kill [^17]. In this diagram:
  "Nein" to `End_AnliegenVerworfen`.
- Problem valid, solution failed: pivot, the story is reworked [^17][^19]. Unclear or only weak evidence:
  run another experiment [^15][^18]. The diagram has no extra edge for these two. Project decision: a pivot goes through "Nein" with the
  pivot noted in `99_uebergabe.md`; for another experiment the checkpoint stays open until the PO brings the new
  test or spike result.

**Recording the decision**:

- A learning card records hypothesis, evidence and the decision with its next action [^20]; the notebook's
  answer gives the four lines "We believed that... / We observed... / From that we learned that... /
  Therefore, we will..." [store-phase-2-klaerung-5].
- State the confidence level, i.e. how strong the evidence is for supporting or refuting the hypothesis [^21].
- Archive negative results too, so nobody repeats the experiment [^22][^23].
- Share the recommendation with stakeholders [^24].

**Pitfalls the notebook names**:

- Weak data: measuring what people say [^4]; deciding on one experiment with weak evidence [^25].
- Confirmation bias, discarding contradicting evidence [^10]; HiPPO decisions and "executing without
  evidence" [^26].
- No success metrics before the test, so the result is interpreted afterwards [^27].
- Helping the test user instead of letting them fail [^28].
- Testing without deciding: "The goal is not to test and learn. The goal is to decide" [^25]; open-ended
  analysis is limited by a time box and evidence-driven debate instead of opinion [^29].

**Not answered by the sources.** No universal share of users that must confirm for the story to pass; the
thresholds in `triage-klaerung-1` come from single sources' examples. No template for a spike findings
report beyond acceptance criteria and recommendation. No rule for weighing effort against value ("acceptable
cost") in numbers. The sources describe a team or stakeholder review, not who exactly decides in a
PO-team setting.
