## Product-Methodik: Definition of Ready

_Ort: notebook:The Product - Business Design_

_Footnotes name the notebook source. The verbatim quotes and FAQ references stay with the generation output (`knowledge/`), which is not installed._

**Boundary test**: a well-formed story has its deliverable ("I want…") in the team's zone of control and
its user need ("In order to…") in the team's sphere of influence. A story that does not fit this
pattern is to be investigated [^1]. When the user need itself sits in the team's zone of control, the
story is "effectively a task without risk" and is fake, micro or misleading [^2].

**Fake story**: a task for the delivery team dressed as a story ("As a QA, in order to test faster, I want
the database server restarts to be automated"). Detection:

- zone-of-control test above [^2];
- role check: a developer or QA role, a system component ("As an LDAP server…") or the generic "As a user…" [^3];
- acceptance-criteria test: ask stakeholders for acceptance criteria; for a task like a server clean-up
  nobody can give a business test [^4][^5].

**Micro-story**: a very small piece of a large business story that carries no risk of its own. Acceptable
for the short term, but track the whole hierarchy and judge it by the larger piece [^2]; in medium- or
long-term plans replace the group with one larger item [^6].

**Misleading story**: names a solution, not the user need (queries optimised "to run reports faster"
while the operator really wants to resolve customer data discrepancies); rewrite around the real need [^7].

**Orphan story**: a deliverable that cannot be attached to a branch of a hierarchical backlog or impact map
(goal, actor, impact, deliverable) or of a story map. It often comes from a reverse-engineered feature idea
with an invented generic role [^8][^9]. Also an orphan: a story whose beneficiary is outside the roles
pre-selected for the current milestone [^10].

**Remedies**

| Finding | Remedy | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fake story | Throw it out of the story backlog; manage legitimate technical work (server clean-up, library upgrade, refactoring) from a separate, dedicated time budget instead of competing with business stories | [^5][^6][^11] |
| Fake story that needs research | Reframe as a learning story with an explicit goal, acceptance criteria and a time box | [^12] |
| Misleading story | Replace; rewrite the "In order to…" and look for the root cause behind the requested fix | [^6][^7] |
| Micro-story | Keep short-term; consolidate in longer-term plans; discard the whole hierarchy if the parts do not achieve the objective | [^2][^6] |
| Orphan story | Move to "not now" or drop as scope creep; if it only fits another milestone, move or split it | [^9][^10][^13] |

**Not answered by the notebook**: how to decide between "not now" and discard for an orphan story beyond
the milestone and impact-map fit, and who decides; the sources describe the practices, not a decision
authority.
