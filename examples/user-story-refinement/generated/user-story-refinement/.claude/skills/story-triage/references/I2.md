## Product-Methodik: Eingang & Triage

_Ort: notebook:The Product - Business Design_

_Footnotes name the notebook source. The verbatim quotes and FAQ references stay with the generation output (`knowledge/`), which is not installed._

Task "Verwandte Stories & Epics im Backlog suchen". What to do with the result (gateway "Wie hängt das
Anliegen mit dem Backlog zusammen?") is in `domain-knowledge.md`; this file covers where to look
and how to match.

**Where to look.** The notebook names five places:

1. **User story map.** Horizontal axis: the steps of a high-level user activity (the backbone).
   Vertical axis: delivery order, with release slices [^1].
2. **Impact map.** Four levels: business goal, actors, impacts (behaviour changes), deliverables
   (stories, epics) [^2].
3. **Opportunity / discovery backlog.** Columns from "Opportunity" through "in Discovery/Problem
   Research", "in Ideation", "in Delivery" to "in Validation", plus "Trash" for rejected items [^3].
4. **Delivery backlog.** "Backlog", "Open" (current sprint), "In progress/Doing", "Code Review",
   "QA" [^4].
5. **Living documentation.** Specifications and tests grouped by functional area describe what is done
   and live; completed stories are thrown away [^5].

**Search order** (the notebook's synthesis across sources, not one source's fixed sequence):

1. Place the feedback: which role or segment, and which activity step on the story-map backbone or
   which actor and behaviour change on the impact map. Work from the problem statement, not from a
   solution card [^6].
2. Check the living documentation: is the behaviour already shipped? Past stories are a poor reference
   because they show change over time, not the current state [^7].
3. Check open, in-progress and in-QA items and the top release slice for a story that already covers
   it.
4. Check the unscheduled backlog, open epics and the opportunity backlog for a theme the feedback fits.
5. Decide duplicate, overlap or gap:
   - Overlap: stories sharing the same underlying work should be combined for planning (example: save
     search results to XML vs. HTML) [^8].
   - Scope fit: a request that fits no branch of the impact map is probably scope creep or "not now" [^9].
   - Clusters of small related stories are distilled into one bigger story or epic card; the small
     ones become bullet points in its description [^10].

**Possible outcomes** (as listed by the notebook): update the open story (its acceptance criteria or
questions) instead of a new ticket [^11]; cluster into an epic or theme [^10]; a new story card placed
under the matching user activity or impact branch; resolved because it already exists; or "Trash" /
"not now" [^3].

**Pitfalls.**

- A flat backlog of hundreds of cards: "story card hell" hides the meaning of the cards and the
  product vision [^12].
- Stale items: stories without a "best before" date quietly turn into emergencies; a backlog also
  becomes a dumping ground for expired, untested ideas [^13].
- An uncoordinated "stream of consciousness" backlog with no business justification per story [^14].

**Terms.**

- *Epic*: a big story that is the right size for business or user talks but too big for development;
  it must be split before it enters a sprint [^15][^16].
- *User activity*: a high-level step that groups tasks around a common goal; the activities form the
  backbone of a story map [^17].
- *Story map*: grid of user activities (horizontal) and delivery order (vertical) [^1].
- *Impact map*: mind map that connects deliverable scope to business goals and assumptions on four
  levels [^2].
- *Opportunity backlog*: the stack of stories not yet decided for build [^18].

**Not covered by the notebook.** Concrete search techniques (keywords, tags, similarity criteria) and
how to search when no story map or impact map exists. Say so to the user and fall back on the epics and
the opportunity backlog.
