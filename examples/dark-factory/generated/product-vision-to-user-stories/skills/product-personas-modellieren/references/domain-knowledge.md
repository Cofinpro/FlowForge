# Domain knowledge — User Roles & Personas modellieren

- Identify user roles across the interaction space first, then condense interview facts into personas; write for a concrete role like "a Geographic Searcher", not "a user" [^1].
- Pass: concrete role label; built only from quotes and observed behaviour; 1–3 JTBD, top 2 pains, top 2 gains, one trigger situation [^1][^2].
- Pitfall: the mythical generic user — generic roles are a get-out-of-jail-free card for pet features and unjustified scope [^2].
- Gedächtnis §9.3: if the evidence mix of personas/jtbd has less than 50 % 🔗, G-P2 routes to "more research" (back to SP2.1).
- §6: mixed evidence takes the strongest level (🔗 > 🤖 > 🧠) and lists all references.

[^1]: "User Stories Applied" (Cohn 2004) — "Rather than writing stories like 'A user can restrict job searches…' you can write 'A Geographic Searcher can restrict his job searches…'"
[^2]: "Fifty Quick Ideas to Improve Your User Stories" (Adzic et al. 2014) — "Using generic roles is often a get-out-of-jail-free card for people who want to push in pet features, or unjustified scope."
