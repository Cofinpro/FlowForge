# Domain knowledge — Technische Unsicherheit als Spike auslagern

**Spike definition.** A spike has a timebox, a question, and acceptance criteria defined in advance [^1][^2].

**Why timebox.** Before performing a spike, clearly define the acceptance criteria and time box — otherwise spikes turn into never-ending research projects [^1].

**Estimable.** INVEST requires a story to be estimable [^2]; larger or unclear stories carry greater uncertainty [^3]. A spike restores estimability; it does not replace splitting.

**Role (Gedächtnis §4).** The architect (ARC) owns spikes and size classes and needs the target architecture/platform from the idea brief (§14); if that field was inferred it is an ASM.

**DoR link.** The Definition of Ready requires no open blockers or un-spiked risks [^2][^1].

[^1]: "Testing Business Ideas" (Bland, Osterwalder 2019) — "Before performing a spike, clearly define the acceptance criteria and time box … These can turn into never-ending research projects if left unchecked."
[^2]: "User Stories Applied" (Cohn 2004) — "A good story is: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable to users or customers, Estimatable, Small, Testable … Bill Wake (2003a) refers to this as 'slicing the cake.' Each story must have a little from each layer."
[^3]: "The Lean Product Playbook" (Olsen) — "Small: Good stories tend to be small in scope. Larger stories will have greater uncertainty … Testable: A good story provides enough information to make it clear how to test that the story is 'done'."
