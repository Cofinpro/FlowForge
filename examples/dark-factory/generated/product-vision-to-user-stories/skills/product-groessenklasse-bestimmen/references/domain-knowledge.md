# Domain knowledge — Groessenklasse bestimmen (S/M/L)

**Human-role replacement (binding, Gedächtnis §4).** Story points become a size class S/M/L with reasoning. INVEST "Small" means ≤ M; L forces splitting.

**Small and Estimable.** Good stories tend to be small in scope; larger stories will have greater uncertainty [^1]. INVEST demands both Estimable and Small [^2].

**Spikes restore estimability.** When uncertainty blocks sizing, a spike with timebox, question and predefined acceptance criteria is the tool [^3].

**DoR.** Size ≤ M and no open blockers or un-spiked risks are part of the Definition of Ready [^2][^3].

[^1]: "The Lean Product Playbook" (Olsen) — "Small: Good stories tend to be small in scope. Larger stories will have greater uncertainty … Testable: A good story provides enough information to make it clear how to test that the story is 'done'."
[^2]: "User Stories Applied" (Cohn 2004) — "A good story is: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable to users or customers, Estimatable, Small, Testable … Bill Wake (2003a) refers to this as 'slicing the cake.' Each story must have a little from each layer."
[^3]: "Testing Business Ideas" (Bland, Osterwalder 2019) — "Before performing a spike, clearly define the acceptance criteria and time box … These can turn into never-ending research projects if left unchecked."
