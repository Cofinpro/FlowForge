# Domain knowledge — Synthetische Interviews durchfuehren

- The interview follows the four-stage funnel: qualifier → story about past behaviour → problem ranking and today's workaround → solution shown last [^1][^2].
- Problem ranking (adopted 2026-09-25): the top 3 problems, shuffled per persona, are ranked by each persona from its own experience [^1]. If the core problem is ranked last, or not at all, by most target personas, that is a kill signal for G-P2 — a resounding "meh" means a clear disconnect [^3].
- Transcript quality is judged by past-behaviour questioning without leading questions [^4] and by the absence of a pitch or guided tour before the last third [^5].
- Gedächtnis §7.4: one transcript T-<nn>_P-<nn> per persona with guide version and model role; the persona may say "don't know" and the interviewer must not reinterpret it.
- §6: panel answers are synthetic evidence 🤖; they count for gates in dark mode but are always labelled as such.
- §9.2: at G-P2 only the 4 target users form the majority; Contrarian and Verweigerer objections must each be answered or turned into a risk flag.

[^1]: "Testing Business Ideas" (Bland, Osterwalder 2019) — "List the top three customer jobs, pains, and gains. Interviewee ranks them based on personal experiences."
[^2]: "Lean UX" (Gothelf, Seiden) — "First, try to identify if the customer is in your target audience. Then, try to confirm any problem hypotheses … if you have a prototype or mockup with you, show this last."
[^3]: "Lean Analytics" (Croll, Yoskovitz) — "Test the problem by getting the subject to rank the problems … you might get a resounding 'meh,' in which case there's a clear disconnect."
[^4]: "The Product Book" (Product School 2017) — "always aim for actual instead of ideal-self questions—i.e., ask what customers have done, not what they might do. Also avoid leading and loaded questions."
[^5]: "UX for Lean Startups" (Klein) — "Don't Give a Guided tour … inexperienced moderators wanting to give way too much information about the product up front."
