# Domain knowledge — Was von Wie trennen

**Negotiable.** A story is negotiable; the card is a placeholder for a conversation, not a contract [^1] — keep How out of the What so it stays negotiable.

**No UI assumptions.** Connextra stories are written in active voice, for one user, without UI assumptions [^1].

**Misleading stories.** A misleading story describes a solution instead of the need [^2].

**Small vs. Valuable.** Technical stories without an outcome are the typical symptom of choosing Small over Valuable [^2].

**DoR (gate dor).** Connextra with a concrete role; INVEST fulfilled; at least one AC; low-fi wireframe as text or Mermaid sketch; no open blockers or un-spiked risks; size ≤ M; terms as in the glossary [^1][^3].

[^1]: "User Stories Applied" (Cohn 2004) — "A good story is: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable to users or customers, Estimatable, Small, Testable … Bill Wake (2003a) refers to this as 'slicing the cake.' Each story must have a little from each layer."
[^2]: "Fifty Quick Ideas to Improve Your User Stories" (Adzic et al. 2014) — "Fake stories are those about the needs of delivery team members … create small conversations that involve at least one person representing each of the development, testing and analysis roles."
[^3]: "Testing Business Ideas" (Bland, Osterwalder 2019) — "Before performing a spike, clearly define the acceptance criteria and time box … These can turn into never-ending research projects if left unchecked."
